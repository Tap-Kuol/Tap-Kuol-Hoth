About

From Bentiu to Cairo, one step at a time.

My journey from South Sudan to a degree in computer and information sciences — and what it taught me.

  • RésuméDownload my CV

I’m Tap Kuol Hoth, a graduate of the Faculty of Computer and Information Sciences at Ain Shams University in Cairo. My journey here started a long way away — in Bentiu, Unity State, South Sudan.

I began school in 2002, learning to read and write in my mother tongue, Nuer (Thok Naath). In July 2004 I moved into English-language education at Yoangang Primary School, a historic religious school in Bentiu. From 2006 to 2011 I studied at Dandok Primary School, and in 2012 I completed Primary 8 at Great Upper Nile Primary School in Bentiu town, the capital of Unity State.

In July 2013 I started at Bentiu National Secondary School. Only months later, on 15 December 2013, conflict broke out in South Sudan and my studies were interrupted. On 21 August 2014 I arrived in Khartoum, Sudan, where I went back to secondary school at Aliab Secondary School in 2015. In February 2018 I earned my Sudan Secondary Certificate.

In September 2018 I moved to Cairo, Egypt, and in September 2019 I was admitted to the Faculty of Computer and Information Sciences at Ain Shams University. Programming was hard at first: computer science wasn’t taught at the primary and secondary schools I attended in South Sudan or Sudan, so I was starting from zero. In the faculty’s general system I studied the full range of computing subjects — software engineering, algorithms and data structures, database management, artificial intelligence, cryptography, network security and electronics, programming in C++, C#, Java and Python, plus calculus and physics — before specialising in Information Systems in my fourth year.

In my first year, my team of four and I built Rover-1, a robotic car programmed in C++ that avoids obstacles on its own — the moment I saw that code could control something real. I graduated on 2 July 2024, and on 23 November 2025 I joined the Tech Department at CardoO, an Egyptian smart-devices and IoT company, as a Software Engineer.

Every step — from Bentiu to Khartoum to Cairo — taught me to adapt quickly, keep learning, and finish what I start.
