Rover-1
Robotics · 2019 · First-year team of 4

Rover-1 — obstacle-avoiding robotic car

Rover-1 is a four-wheel-drive robotic car that my team of four built in our first year. It finds its own way around obstacles. Its servo-mounted ultrasonic sensor scans ahead, and when something blocks the path the car turns left, turns right or reverses. It can also be controlled from a laptop. Programmed in C++.

  • C++
  • Robotics
  • Embedded systems
  • Ultrasonic sensor
  • Obstacle avoidance
Read the case study
The goal
Build a small car that can drive itself without crashing: notice when something is in its way and choose a new direction on its own, while still letting us take control from a laptop.
How we built it
Our team of four assembled a four-wheel-drive chassis with four DC motors, mounted an ultrasonic distance sensor on a servo at the front, and wired everything to the controller board. We wrote the control program in C++. It reads the distance ahead and, when the path is blocked, uses the servo to look left and right before deciding to turn left, turn right or back up.
How it decides
  1. Measure — the ultrasonic sensor checks the distance ahead
  2. Decide — the C++ program compares it with a safe distance
  3. Path clear? Keep driving. Blocked? Stop
  4. Scan — the servo turns the sensor left and right
  5. Act — turn toward the clearer side, or reverse if both are blocked
The result
A working car, built in 2019, that avoids obstacles on its own and can also be driven from a laptop. It brought together mechanics, electronics and software in one project.
